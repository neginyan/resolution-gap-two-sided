The resolution gap $g$ measures how far an observer who sees a pendulum's phase space through a Gaussian blur of width $\sigma$ is from
resolving the arrow of time near the hyperbolic top. A companion paper (paper~E) showed that no $\sigma$-uniform gap exists, found the
\emph{strip} family whose smallest gap follows a logarithmic law, and conjectured that $g\ge c/\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}$ for all states.
Here we prove the logarithmic law with the sharp constant on a larger family, the \emph{periodic windows}: a Gaussian state whose density
carries, inside a narrow window, any even periodic profile in the momentum-like direction, from a single cosine (the strip) to a comb of
thin teeth. Under explicit assumptions collected in one place, we prove
$(1-\bar\varepsilon)(1-1.7\,\ln\ln/\ln)\le g_{\min}\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}\le(1+|\varepsilon_C|)(1+6.0\,\ln\ln/\ln)$ for every
$10^{-100000}\le\sigma\le10^{-24}$, where $\bar\varepsilon\le0.1$ bounds paper~E's remainder $\varepsilon$ on the class and $\varepsilon_C$ is the remainder of one
explicit comb (numerically $4\times10^{-16}$ at $\sigma=10^{-24}$, the smallest $\sigma$ the exact evaluator reaches). For the limit we prove $\liminf g_{\min}\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}\ge1-\bar\varepsilon$; the matching $\limsup\le1$, and so the limit $1$, is derived, not proved.
The lower side combines an exact window inequality with an upward bootstrap and is certified on $421$ cells covering the range of $\ln(1/\sigma)$
and all $\bar\varepsilon\le0.1$ at once; the upper side follows an explicit comb sequence and is certified with exact exponents and interval arithmetic. At finite $\sigma$ the comb is the strip seen through
a lens $256$ times sharper (a leading-order identity), and numerically it falls below the best Gaussian mixtures already at
$\sigma\approx1.4\times10^{-3}$. Towards general states we prove a decorrelation dichotomy and a three-case split; the remaining case is
reduced to a single conjectured inequality. Every claim carries its status: proof, computer-assisted proof, derivation, numerical, or conjecture.
