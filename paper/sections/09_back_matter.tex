\begin{acknowledgments}
The author used AI assistants in this work. Anthropic Claude (\mbox{claude-opus-5-5}) was used in two separate sessions. In the first session it planned the study and wrote the instructions for each stage, re-ran every verification suite independently, checked key steps by independent calculations (among them the periodic average behind Lemma~\ref{lem:AY1P}, the leading-order amplitude of the comb and the ratio of Proposition~\ref{prop:BE1}, and the lower-side certificate at random interior points of its cells), found that the first lower-side certificate covered only isolated values of $\sigma$ and $\varepsilon$, and reviewed the manuscript. In the second session it carried out the computations, derived the lemmas and the window inequality, wrote the computer-assisted certificates, the evaluation and verification code, and drafted the text. Results were passed between the sessions as written summaries and checked in the first session. Google Gemini (Gemini 4 Argon) and OpenAI ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Luna) independently reviewed the manuscript of version 1.0 and the new appendix of version 1.1, re-checking the derivations, the monotonicity lemma and the quantification of the remainder $\varepsilon$; their comments led to the monotonicity table of Appendix~B, the explicit listing of the assumptions, and a more careful statement of the limit. For version 1.1, the second session found, while extending the proofs, that the window lemmas of v1.0 had treated the Gaussian average over $v$ as an exact period average, wrote the $\theta_\tau$ forms of the lemmas, the narrow-width lemma and the perturbed certificates of Appendix~\ref{app:theta}, and removed the start condition (P8); the first session re-ran and checked these certificates independently. For version 1.2, the second session found that one factor in the certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:BK1} is not monotone, audited every certificate at interior points of its cells, and removed (P5) for strips and weakened it for windows with at most six harmonics (Appendix~\ref{app:P5}); an independent Claude agent re-read the new appendix, and the first session re-ran and checked these certificates. For version 1.3, the second session proved the upper bound of the comb beyond $k_0=240$ with exact leading constants (Appendix~\ref{app:cert}) and extended the removal of (P5) to windows with at most $16$ harmonics (Appendix~\ref{app:FN}); an independent Claude agent audited the asymptotic forms against its own direct evaluation, and the first session re-ran these certificates and made explicit that the comb is in the class only when $|\varepsilon(S'_C)|\le\bar\varepsilon$. The author directed the research, made all decisions on its content, and takes full responsibility for this article.
\end{acknowledgments}

\section*{Data and code availability}
All scripts, stored results, the certificates and the verification code are available at
\url{https://github.com/neginyan/resolution-gap-two-sided} and archived at Zenodo, \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23746555}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.23746555}; a single entry point, \code{verification/run\_all.py}, checks the stored results
against their hashes and recomputes representative cells of every certificate. The computer-assisted parts of the proofs are in
\code{certificates/}: \code{bh\_cert\_cells.py} (with \code{bf\_cert\_p.py}), \code{bh\_tail.py}, \code{bf\_ay\_bootstrap.py}, \code{ba\_ivl.py},
\code{ba\_cert.py}, \code{bf\_cert\_upper.py}, \code{bg\_members.py} and \code{bh\_members\_fc.py}; for v1.1 also \code{bk\_narrow\_lemma.py} and
\code{bl\_narrow80.py} (Lemma~\ref{lem:BK1}), \code{bl\_theta.py} (App.~\ref{app:theta}), \code{bl\_chain.py} (the chain with perturbed inputs),
\code{bl\_bj1.py} (Lemma~\ref{lem:BJ1}) and \code{bm\_bl7.py} (the upper side in $\theta_\tau$ form); for v1.2 also \code{bn\_strip\_x.py},
\code{bp\_strip\_p5.py}, \code{bp\_windowsN.py} and \code{bq\_proofs.py} (App.~\ref{app:P5}); for v1.3 also \code{bs\_upper\_tail.py} and \code{bs\_upper\_tail\_stress.py} (App.~\ref{app:cert}), \code{br\_cells.py} and \code{br\_windows.py} (App.~\ref{app:FN}); each reruns on a single core in under an hour. The audit of
App.~\ref{app:cert} is \code{bp\_audit.py} (about 40 minutes; \code{run\_all.py} runs a reduced version by default).
A notebook in \code{demo/} shows the two-sided band and the comb--strip relation of Proposition~\ref{prop:BE1}.
