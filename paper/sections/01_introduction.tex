\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An observer who sees a mechanical system only through a blur cannot always tell the direction of time. A sharp criterion for when Gaussian
coarse-graining produces a coarse-grained arrow of time was given in Ref.~\cite{paperA}, and the \emph{resolution gap} of nonlinear flows was introduced in
Ref.~\cite{paperB}. Paper~E~\cite{paperE} made this
quantitative for the simplest unstable motion, the pendulum near its hyperbolic top, and studied the \emph{resolution gap}
$g$ (the gap per unit blur, Sec.~\ref{sec:setting}) over all phase-space states. Its main findings were:
(i) $g$ is \emph{not} bounded below uniformly in the blur width $\sigma$: a family of ``ripple strips'' has $g_{\min}(\sigma)\to0$;
(ii) on that family $g_{\min}(\sigma)$ follows a logarithmic law, found numerically as $1.02/\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)-23.4}$ over
$10^{-2}\ge\sigma\ge10^{-96}$; (iii) unconditional log-law theorems on several families and a conjecture (Conjecture~1 of paper~E)
that $g\ge c/\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)+C}$ for all states under its confinement hypotheses. Paper~E listed as an open problem to derive the
strip law analytically.

\paragraph*{What this paper does.}
We prove the logarithmic law \emph{with the sharp constant} on a family that contains the strips: the \emph{periodic windows}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:windows}). A periodic window is a Gaussian state that carries, inside a window of width $\ell\sim\sqrt\sigma$ in the
slow direction $w$, an arbitrary $2\pi$-periodic profile $f$ in the fast direction $v$; the strip is $f=1+c\cos t$, the comb is a
train of thin teeth whose fundamental amplitude approaches the largest value $2$ that a positive profile allows.
The central result is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Main result} (Theorem~\ref{thm:BF1} in Sec.~\ref{sec:upper}) \caproofst{App.~\ref{app:cert}}. Under the assumptions (P0), (P2)--(P7) of Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} (for windows with at most $20$ harmonics without (P5); App.~\ref{app:FN}),
$(1-\bar\varepsilon)(1-1.7\tfrac{\ln\ln(1/\sigma)}{\ln(1/\sigma)})\le g_{\min}^{P}\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}
\le(1+|\varepsilon(S'_C(\sigma))|)(1+6.0\,\tfrac{\ln\ln(1/\sigma)}{\ln(1/\sigma)})$ for every $10^{-100000}\le\sigma\le10^{-24}$, where every state of the class has
$|\varepsilon|\le\bar\varepsilon\le0.1$, $S'_C(\sigma)$ is an explicit comb, and $|\varepsilon(S'_C(\sigma))|\le5.62\times10^{-7}$ (App.~\ref{app:epscomb}).
Below $10^{-100000}$ the lower side is proved analytically (Lemma~\ref{lem:BH2}) and the comb's bound holds for every $\sigma\le10^{-24}$, so
$1-\bar\varepsilon\le\liminf g_{\min}\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}\le\limsup g_{\min}\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}\le1$ on every class with $\bar\varepsilon\ge5.62\times10^{-7}$, and
$g(S'_C)\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}\to1$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:limit}). The limit is $1$ in this sense: both ends tend to $1$ as the class bound $\bar\varepsilon$ does.
\end{quote}

Here $\varepsilon(\rho)$ is paper~E's remainder of its Theorem~2, a quantity of each state $\rho$ that we carry exactly as paper~E does. The lower
side assumes a bound $|\varepsilon(\rho)|\le\bar\varepsilon\le0.1$ on the whole class; the upper side holds on one explicit comb $S'_C(\sigma)$ and carries
that state's own $|\varepsilon(S'_C(\sigma))|$, which we bound by $276.4\,k_0^{-2}e^{-3k_0^2/2}(1.103)\le5.62\times10^{-7}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:G1}, v1.4; numerically it is at the rounding level, $4\times10^{-16}$ at $10^{-24}$).

\paragraph*{Version~1.1.}
Version~1.0 treated the Gaussian average over the background's $v$-envelope and the average over one ripple period as equal. For states whose
background $v$-width is at most a few $\sigma$ this needs a correction: the Gaussian average is a period average weighted by a wrapped normal
$\theta_\tau$, $\tau=k'^2V$, and differs from the plain one by a relative $\delta_\tau=2\sum_ne^{-n^2\tau/2}$. Version~1.1 supplies the correction:
Lemma~\ref{lem:BK1} shows that such narrow states have a large gap, so that every state with $g<G$ has $\tau>80$ ($\delta_\tau<10^{-17}$), and
App.~\ref{app:theta} restates every window lemma in $\theta_\tau$ form with explicit remainders; the certificates were re-run with the input lemmas
perturbed by a relative $10^{-6}$. The bounds of the theorems are unchanged; the standing condition $L_w\le\sigma^{-1/2}$, already used in v1.0, is now listed as (P0), and the limit is stated with what is proved and what is derived kept apart. Version~1.1 also removes the a-priori condition (P8) of v1.0
($F_c\ge1.02G$), which now follows from (P0)--(P7) (Lemma~\ref{lem:BJ1}).

\paragraph*{Version~1.2.}
Version~1.2 removes the condition (P5), $x\ge1$, for strips: a strip whose ripple period is longer than the blur has $g\ge0.134$, by a four-case
argument of which two cases are computer-assisted (App.~\ref{app:P5}, Theorem~\ref{thm:E6}). For windows with at most six harmonics (P5) is weakened to
$x\ge10^{-12}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:E8}). In the certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:BK1} one factor is not monotone in $\tau$; with its supremum over each cell
every cell still closes, and the smallest margin is $2.78$ instead of $2.80$; the proof now also states why the cells may be evaluated at $\sigma=10^{-24}$.
Every certificate was audited at more than two million interior points of its cells (App.~\ref{app:cert}); apart from that factor no quantity was
found on the wrong side. The bounds of the theorems are unchanged.

\paragraph*{Version~1.3.}
Version~1.3 proves the upper bound of the comb, $U_P\sqrt\ls\le1+6.0\ln\ls/\ls$, for every $\sigma\le10^{-24}$, not only down to $10^{-100000}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:AZ1P}): beyond the cells, every factor is written as its leading monomial with an exact constant times $(1+Rk_0^{-\nu})$ with an explicit
interval $R$ (App.~\ref{app:cert}). So of the two facts that the limit $1$ needed, $U_P\sqrt\ls\to1$ is now proved and only $\varepsilon(S'_C)\to0$ remains.
It also removes (P5) for windows with at most $16$ harmonics and weakens it to $x\ge10^{-12}$ for at most $20$ harmonics (App.~\ref{app:FN}), with
budgeted harmonic sums and a bound of $\langle\theta_\tau h_t^2\rangle$ for small $\tau$. Finally, it states explicitly that the comb belongs to the class only when $|\varepsilon(S'_C(\sigma))|\le\bar\varepsilon$ (P6); v1.2 left this condition of the middle inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm:BF1} implicit. The bounds of the theorems are unchanged.

\paragraph*{Version~1.4.}
Version~1.4 bounds paper~E's remainder along the comb, $|\varepsilon(S'_C(\sigma))|\le276.4\,k_0^{-2}e^{-3k_0^2/2}(1.103)$ for every $\sigma\le10^{-24}$
(App.~\ref{app:epscomb}): the remainder splits into a part integrated by parts, a third posterior cumulant and a Lagrange remainder bounded independently of the state.
So the comb belongs to every class with $\bar\varepsilon\ge5.62\times10^{-7}$, and the upper limit $1$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:limit} is proved. It also removes the
evenness condition (P1): the parity steps are exact derivatives for every profile with a common envelope, and the three places that used evenness
in an essential way (a Toeplitz factor, a small-$\tau$ bound, and the phase of $X_w$ in case~C) are replaced (App.~\ref{app:FN}). Finally, keeping the
factor $e=e^{-\alpha^2V/2}$ in the $\theta_\tau$ remainders (Lemma~\ref{lem:Fe}) removes (P5) for windows with at most $20$ harmonics.

\paragraph*{Relation to paper~E.}
Paper~E \emph{found} the strip law and stated it as a property of the family it had searched; nothing in it is contradicted here.
Paper~F proves the law from both sides on a family that is larger than the strips, explains the fitted constant $1.02$ as the slow
$O(\ln\ln/\ln)$ approach to the limit $1$ over the fitted range, and shows that within the periodic windows the strip is \emph{not} the
lowest state at finite $\sigma$: the comb is lower, by an exact leading-order rule (``doubling the fundamental's contrast is worth
dividing the blur by $256$'', Proposition~\ref{prop:BE1}). The constant of the limit is the same.

\paragraph*{How the proof sees the state (observer picture).}
The observer learns the fast coordinate $v$ from the ripple, and learns the slow coordinate $w$ from the ripple's edges, because a ripple
that is visible only inside a window must switch off somewhere in $w$. An exact inequality (the window inequality, Theorem~\ref{thm:AT2})
turns this into $g\gtrsim1/(2k)$ for a ripple of fineness $k$. The blur decides how fine a ripple can still be seen: its contrast
decays like $e^{-k^2/2}$ and must beat the background's own $v$-signal, which caps $k^2$ at $\tfrac14\ln(1/\sigma)$.
Together: $g\gtrsim1/\sqrt{\ln(1/\sigma)}$, and a comb sequence reaches it.

\paragraph*{Status labels.}
Every statement carries one of: \proofst{} (a complete proof is given or cited); \status{proof, computer-assisted} (a proof in which
finitely many explicit inequalities are verified by a program with rigorous cell bounds or interval arithmetic; the program is in the
repository); \derivst{} (leading-order analysis without a remainder bound); \numst{} (computation with paper~E's evaluator);
\conjst. Table~\ref{tab:claims} lists the claims.

\paragraph*{Outline.}
Section~\ref{sec:setting} recalls the gap formula and the two exact tools we use for every state (the branch identity and the window
inequality). Section~\ref{sec:windows} defines the periodic windows and the lemmas that carry the strip analysis to them.
Sections~\ref{sec:lower} and~\ref{sec:upper} prove the lower and the upper side and state Theorem~\ref{thm:BF1}.
Section~\ref{sec:finite} describes the family at practical blurs. Section~\ref{sec:general} records what is proved for general states
and the one inequality that is missing. Proofs of the window lemmas and the certificates are in the appendices.
